Privacy notice and terms of use

SAMS films the area around a machine to warn workers before they get hurt. This page explains, in plain words, what it records, why, who can see it, how long it is kept, and what you can ask us to do. It follows Rwanda's Law N° 058/2021 on the protection of personal data and privacy.

1. Who is responsible

The site that installs SAMS is the data controller: it decides to monitor the machine area and answers for the footage. During the capstone pilot the controller is the host site, and the developer (UTERAMAHORO Avellin Bonaparte, ALU) runs the system on its behalf. Questions and requests go to the site safety officer named on the notice posted at the machine.

2. What SAMS records

  • Video from one phone camera pointed at the machine area. Anyone who walks into view is filmed.
  • What the models saw for each moment: person boxes, distance to the machine, protective gear worn or missing, fire, smoke, falls.
  • Machine readings from the board: vibration and whether the machine is running. These are not about people.
  • Operator accounts: username and a hashed password for staff who sign in. SAMS never stores a password in plain text.

SAMS does not recognise faces, store names of filmed workers, or record sound.

3. Why, and what it will never be used for

Footage is used for three things only: the live warning (buzzer and lights), reviewing a CAUTION or DANGER moment afterwards, and improving the safety models.

It must not be used to rate, discipline, or dismiss a worker, or to track attendance or productivity. A SAMS decision is a warning for a person to check, never a final judgement about anyone.

4. Who can see it

  • Only signed-in staff (operator and safety officer) can open the dashboard, archive, alerts and training desk.
  • Video travels from the phone to the server over HTTPS on the site's own Wi-Fi. It is not sent to any cloud service to run.
  • Footage is stored on the site server, in Rwanda. If moments are exported to train a new model, they are exported only after a safety officer has reviewed them, and storing them outside Rwanda needs the authorisation required by Articles 48 and 50 of the law.

5. How long it is kept

Recordings are deleted automatically after {{ retention_days }} days. The only exception is a moment a safety officer has marked Keep for training; its recording stays until that mark is removed or it is deleted by hand.

6. Your rights

  • Be told: a printed notice at the machine says that the area is filmed and links to this page.
  • See the moments in which you appear, with the safety officer.
  • Erase: ask for a recording to be deleted. Staff press Delete recording on that recording, which removes the video, the annotated copy and every saved frame.
  • Object or withdraw consent to your footage being used for training. The moment is marked Exclude and never exported.
  • Complain to the National Cyber Security Authority (Data Protection and Privacy Office) if you think your data was misused.

7. Terms of use for operators

  • SAMS is a research prototype. It can miss a hazard or raise a false alarm. It does not replace machine guards, lock-out procedures, protective equipment or a supervisor.
  • When an input is missing (no camera, no board, a model offline) SAMS shows No decision instead of guessing. Treat that as "not monitored".
  • Post the camera notice before going live. Do not point the camera at toilets, changing areas or places outside the machine zone.
  • Do not share or copy footage outside SAMS. Use the archive, and delete what is no longer needed.
  • SAMS uses open-source components under their own licences, including Ultralytics YOLOv8 (AGPL-3.0); see the project README.
